ABOUT THE TECHNOLOGY

What Is Deepfake Detection?

Deepfake detection is the process of using artificial intelligence and digital forensics to identify whether an image has been generated or manipulated by AI. A trained detection model looks for subtle visual patterns that are difficult to notice with the human eye, including unusual facial textures, inconsistent lighting, unnatural edges, pixel-level artifacts, and other characteristics associated with synthetic or manipulated content. The model then produces a prediction and confidence score indicating how strongly the image matches patterns learned from real and manipulated examples.

WHY IT MATTERS

Why Should We Be Careful With Deepfake Images?

Deepfake images can be used to create false identities, spread misinformation, impersonate people, manipulate evidence, damage reputations, and support online scams. A realistic-looking image is not automatically trustworthy. Before using or sharing suspicious visual content, it is important to verify its source, context, metadata when available, and supporting evidence. Detection tools can help identify suspicious content, but their results should not be treated as absolute proof.

REAL-WORLD PRACTICE

How Deepfake Detection Works in Real Life

In real-world investigations, deepfake detection is usually one part of a larger verification process. An AI detector analyzes the image, while investigators may also examine the original source, file metadata, image history, visual inconsistencies, reverse-image evidence, and other forensic signals. The goal is not simply to label an image as real or fake, but to build enough evidence to make a reliable judgment. This is why a detection score should be interpreted as evidence that supports verification, not as a guarantee.
